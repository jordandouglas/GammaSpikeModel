# Primary sources and scope

Tobias Dieselhorst and Tanja Stadler, *Information on hidden birth events restores identifiability in phylodynamic inference*, [arXiv:2604.17926v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17926v1),20 April 2026. Section 2.3 equations 13–14 and19–21 specify the CGF and cumulants. Theorem 2.4 establishes full-law identifiability using the CGF singularity before its cumulant comparison. The finite unknown-scale classification is a new question formulated here, not a source-declared open conjecture or a counterexample to that theorem. The printed summation index in the theorem is inconsistent with its B+1 conditional law/CGF; this packet follows the consistent formulas.

Douglas et al. (2025), *Evolution is coupled with branching across many granularities of life*, [DOI10.1098/rspb.2025.0182](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2025.0182), and [author GammaSpikeModel software](https://github.com/jordandouglas/GammaSpikeModel). README Spikes specifies shape=spikeShape*(ni+1), scale=spikeMean/spikeShape, while full branch distance also includes gradual evolution. This is the original software, not a verified code repository accompanying the separate Dieselhorst–Stadler theory paper.

A related shifted-count family appears in Amina Shahzadi, Ting Wang, Matthew Parry and Mark Bebbington (2025), *Modelling time-inhomogeneous incomplete records of point processes using variants of hidden Markov models*, [DOI10.1007/s11634-025-00632-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11634-025-00632-x), [institutional primary record](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/handle/10179/73052). Its displayed count gives two required gamma terms, a B+2 relative. We do not claim the random-sum distribution itself is new.

Bounded primary-source/title/model/finite-cumulant searches on 27 October 2026 did not locate an equivalent complete classification. That search is not a priority certification. Standard CGF differentiation, algebraic elimination, monotonicity, polynomial gcds and Sturm isolation are credited as methods. No third-party papers or private correspondence are redistributed.

For ordinary, unshifted compound-Poisson gamma sums, moment inversion is established literature: Ebner–Swan, [arXiv:2304.13179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13179), Example 2 and equation 12, gives three-moment estimators. That Poisson count includes zero contributions and differs from this B+1 law. We do not claim ordinary compound-Poisson inversion as new.
